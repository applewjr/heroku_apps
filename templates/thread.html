Thread

You never see the maze. You only write the rules that walk it.

How to play ▼

You do not solve the maze. You write a program that solves the maze, then watch it run in the dark. The maze is only revealed where your bot has been.

The rules

Your program is an ordered list. Every tick, the bot reads the list from the top and runs the first rule whose conditions are all true. That is the whole language - order is your only control flow, so a rule near the bottom only fires when everything above it fails.

What the bot can sense

Only three things: the walls of the square it is standing on, its own memory of where it has already walked, and its flags. It has no map. Directions are relative to the way it faces, so right means the bot's right, not east.

Memory comes in two kinds. A way is the gap between two squares, and the bot counts how many times it has walked through each one. A square is a place, and it counts how often it has stood there. Which of the two you lean on is up to you.

Ticks

One rule fires per tick and every tick costs a step, including turns and flag changes. Moving into a wall turns the bot to face that way but gets it nowhere - and still costs a step. The run ends when the bot reaches the exit, when no rule matches, when it stops finding new ground, or when it runs out of steps.

Scoring

Two numbers: steps taken and rules used. Solving is the floor. Solving in fewer steps, with fewer rules, is the game. Gauntlet runs your program against five freshly generated mazes it has never seen, which is the real test of whether you wrote a strategy or just got lucky.
