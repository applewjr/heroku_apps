Frequently Asked Questions

What is Wordle?

Wordle is the New York Times' free daily word puzzle. You have 6 attempts to guess a secret 5-letter word. After each guess, tiles turn green (right letter, right position), yellow (right letter, wrong position), or gray (letter not in the word).

Is this Wordle solver free to use?

Yes, completely free. No account, no sign-up, and no download required. Enter your guess results and get the best next word instantly.

How do I use this Wordle solver?

Click a suggested word to fill the row, then click each letter box to cycle its color to match what Wordle showed you: gray for not in the word, yellow for wrong position, green for correct position. The solver updates its suggestions after each row.

What is the solve rate?

The solver achieves a 99.9% solve rate across all 5,710 possible Wordle answers in backtesting, averaging 3.8 guesses (3.7 on common words). Every suggestion is scored by how much its colors would narrow down the answers still possible.

What strategy does the solver use?

For each guess it could suggest, the solver works out the colors that guess would get against every answer still possible, and scores it by how much those colors would narrow the answers down (its expected information). Common words count as likelier answers, since daily answers are everyday words. The Picks are the best guesses that could still be the answer, so they always fit your hints, hard mode included. On the final guess, or with two answers left, the most common word goes first.

What are Alt Picks?

Alt Picks appear when a word that can't be the answer would narrow the answers down more than any word that can, for example when the answers left differ by a letter or two (WAVER / RAVER / SAVER). One guess then tests several uncertain letters at once, instead of trying the look-alikes one by one.

About this tool

This is an unofficial solver for the New York Times Wordle puzzle, provided for assistance and entertainment purposes only. Candidates are ranked by the information their colors would reveal. Word popularity scores (used to weigh likely answers, and on the final guess) are derived from the wordfreq open-source Python library. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the New York Times.
