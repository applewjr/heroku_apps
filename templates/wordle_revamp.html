Frequently Asked Questions

What is Wordle?

Wordle is the New York Times' free daily word puzzle. You have 6 attempts to guess a secret 5-letter word. After each guess, tiles turn green (right letter, right position), yellow (right letter, wrong position), or gray (letter not in the word).

Is this Wordle solver free to use?

Yes, completely free. No account, no sign-up, and no download required. Enter your guess results and get the best next word instantly.

How do I use this Wordle solver?

Click a suggested word to fill the row, then click each letter box to cycle its color to match what Wordle showed you: gray for not in the word, yellow for wrong position, green for correct position. The solver updates its suggestions after each row.

What is the solve rate?

The solver achieves a 97% solve rate across all 5,710 possible Wordle answers in backtesting. It uses an information-first strategy that adapts as guesses run out, switching from candidate guesses to eliminator words when the remaining option pool is too large to win by guessing alone.

What strategy does the solver use?

The solver uses an information-first approach that adapts as guesses run out. Early on, it picks words that eliminate as many letters as possible. When candidates narrow to a similar cluster, it automatically switches to an eliminator word from the full word list, one that resolves several uncertain letters at once rather than cycling through similar guesses. As guesses run low, it becomes more aggressive: the eliminator threshold drops and the search triggers even when candidates aren't obviously similar, because arriving at the last guess with many options is a losing position. On the final guess, it prioritizes the most commonly used words to maximize the chance of a correct pick.

What are Alt Picks?

Alt Picks appear when the top suggestions all differ by only 1 or 2 letters (e.g. WAVER / RAVER / SAVER). Instead of guessing similar words one by one, an Alt Pick is a word from the full dictionary chosen to test several of those uncertain letters in a single guess, helping you narrow down the answer faster.

About this tool

This is an unofficial solver for the New York Times Wordle puzzle, provided for assistance and entertainment purposes only. Candidate ranking uses a custom letter-frequency scoring model. Word popularity scores (used on the final guess) are derived from the wordfreq open-source Python library. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the New York Times.
