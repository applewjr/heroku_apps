Boards

How boards and answers are served

A new 4x3 comes out at each player's own midnight (the game goes by the device's date), so boards are kept by the player's date, and two dates are live at once around the world. This tab shows Pacific tomorrow (players ahead of you), today and the two days before.

Boards players typed are logged once they use the solver on them. A board is its nine words in any order. It's served once {{ serve_networks }} players enter the same words that date; players on one network count as one. A board loaded from today's boards counts only once it's changed. Your approval comes first, then the players' boards, most players first, at most {{ todays_boards_max }}. A board first seen on an earlier date (an archive replay) isn't served unless you approve it for that date. Served boards are in alphabetical order, never a finished game's layout.

Answers: your approval's groups show to every player of that board as Checked, and its names as themes. Without one, a group {{ confirm_networks }}+ players marked right (more right than missed) shows as Confirmed by players, and one {{ confirm_networks }}+ marked as a miss is never suggested. Marks are what the game said: right, one away, not even close.

Approve: Enter your board below, or click a board to open it in the editor with your answer, the players' groups or the solver's filled in. Undo takes your approval back on every date (the players' boards and marks decide again). Remove deletes that date's entries of the board, approvals too. Each answer also tunes the solver, from {{ min_tuning }} answers on (Accuracy tab).

Accuracy

What these numbers are

Every board you answered in the last {{ accuracy_days }} days, against the solver's own first pass on it from the moment just before you first answered it (no marks, nothing confirmed). The weights it ran with were fit on your earlier answers only, so each line is a fair test. The totals per label show whether "sure" really means sure.

The weights: from {{ min_tuning }} answers on, the solver refits them after each approval or Undo to make your answers most likely, pulled toward the defaults so a few boards can only nudge them. They can re-balance the signals the solver has; a kind of link it can't see (trivia, wordplay) stays a guess until its data grows.
