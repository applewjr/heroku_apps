How to use ▼
1. Pick the category. Choose the closest match to the game's category clue, or Not sure / other.

2. Build the board. Tap each word's length in order: for a board of 3, 3 and 4 letters, tap 3, 3, 4. The board wraps into rows like the game's, so you can check yours matches. Tap a word to change it, ⌫ removes one, then tap Done. Count letter tiles only: skip apostrophes and other punctuation.

3. Copy what the game shows. Tap a tile, then a letter. A called letter shows up everywhere it is in the answer, so fill in every tile that shows it. Switch to ✗ Mark misses and tap each letter the game said isn't there.

4. Get a hint. Best next letter shows the consonant most likely to be in the answer, and whether a vowel is worth buying. Still stuck? Open Show possible answers: each one stays hidden until you tap Show letter or Reveal. Works for older puzzles too.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Guess The Phrase?

Guess The Phrase is Bored Panda's daily word puzzle in the style of Wheel of Fortune. Each day has one hidden phrase and a category clue. You spin a wheel to set a point value, call consonants, buy vowels for 250 points, and solve the phrase as early as you can for a bigger bonus. Landing on the flame burns your banked points.

How does this solver help without spoiling the answer?

It works from the board as you see it, never from the day's answer, so nothing is given away until you ask. First it suggests the letter most likely to be in the answer and whether a vowel is worth buying. If you are still stuck, Show possible answers lists phrases that fit your board with every hidden letter masked. Tap Show letter to uncover one letter at a time, or Reveal to see the whole phrase.

How do I enter the board?

Tap each word's length in order: for a board of 3, 3 and 4 letters, tap 3, 3 and 4. The board builds as you tap and wraps into rows the way the game's does, so you can check it matches. Count letter tiles only and skip apostrophes and other punctuation. On a computer you can type the board as it looks instead, with a ? for each blank. Then tap Done, tap a tile and a letter to fill in each letter the game shows, and switch to Mark misses to tap the letters it said are not in the phrase. A called letter appears everywhere it occurs in the answer, so fill in every tile that shows it.

Which letter should I call first?

The classic opening is R, S, T, L, N and E, the most common letters in English phrases. The solver does better than a fixed order: it checks every known phrase that fits your word lengths and category, and suggests the consonant most likely to be in yours, along with how many tiles it should uncover.

Is it worth buying a vowel?

A vowel costs 250 points, so it pays when it is likely to land. The solver shows the vowel most likely to be in the answer and its chance. A vowel with a high chance can break a tricky board open, so buy one early if it gives you the anchor words you need.

What is a Phrase Mashup?

A Phrase Mashup joins two phrases that share a word, the way Before and After puzzles do: FIRE TRUCK and TRUCK STOP become FIRE TRUCK STOP. Choose Phrase mashup as the category and the solver builds these combinations from its phrase list.

Can I use it for older puzzles?

Yes. The solver only needs the board in front of you, so it works the same for today's puzzle and for any earlier one you are catching up on in Bored Panda's archive.

Why isn't my answer in the list?

The phrase list is built from public data: movie, song, TV and book titles, famous people, places, landmarks, animals, food, proverbs and idioms. Puns, song lyrics and made-up phrases may not be in it. When nothing fits, the solver builds guesses word by word instead, and once you have solved the puzzle you can add the answer for the next player.
