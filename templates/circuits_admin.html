Today's Boards

How boards are served

The game day is Puzzmo's: the date in America/Chicago, for every player. Puzzmo and Yahoo Games (powered by Puzzmo) each have their own daily board, both linked at the top, so a day can have more than one real board.

Boards players typed are logged once they use the solver on them. A board is served once {{ serve_majority }} players enter the same cells (each arrow kept if more than half of them drew it), or as soon as {{ serve_identical }} players' boards match exactly. Only finished boards count: a box, and every tile on an arrow. A player's board only ever gains arrows, and a board loaded from today's boards counts only once it's changed. Players on one network count as one. Served are the boards offered that day, for each player to pick theirs: your approvals first, then boards the players settled, most players first, at most {{ todays_boards_max }}.

Approve: enter your game's board with Enter your board below, or press Add for a site on a board you entered yourself on /circuits (Add copies the most recently updated board with those cells, its time under the buttons, and approves nothing if a newer one came in before you pressed it). Each site has its own approval. Undo takes a site's approval back and leaves the players' boards alone. Remove deletes that day's entries of the board, approvals too.

Answers: an approved board gets a box per answer, filled with what you saved, else a word {{ entered_prefill }}+ players kept, else the solver's pick. Saved answers show to players as checked on that board. Once saved, the solver's misses and the pairs your answers need that the data lacks are listed; Add teaches the solver a pair.

Added Pairs

About added pairs

Compounds you taught the solver: the first word written first (SNOW + BALL). The solver counts each as sure, and a single word in one can be an answer even if it's rarer than the solver would guess on its own. One side must be a word the data knows; the other can be a tile's whole text even if the data doesn't know it (SHOW AND), and the solver then knows that tile by it. Remove takes one back.
